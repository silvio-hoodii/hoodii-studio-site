Your other two pointsVariants, and how we know the list is complete

You were right that the completeness claim was circular. I measured coverage against the 34 exercises already in the file, which can only ever come back complete, because those exercises define the muscles that get counted.

You cannot prove a list complete against every exercise that exists. But your gym is finite and already written down: 6 lifting zones, 21 stations. So that is what it gets measured against now, and the audit found real holes, including a movement I had left out entirely (hip abduction, which the squat does not cover because it is glute medius).

The catalogue now holds 25 movements and 103 variants, each with its implement, its zone and its station. Every station in the gym is reachable. Every one of the 76 alternate ids scattered through the old per-slot lists now resolves to a catalogue entry.

Your straight-arm pulldown example, and the twist in it

It was real and it is now fixed on both days: the overhead triceps extension runs at the same pulley as the pulldown in front of it.

But the same-zone version does not work, and the validator caught me trying it. The cable section is three separate columns. A seated row holds the low pulley and a cable lateral raise needs the adjustable one, so they cannot share a rest window at all. Same for the shoulder press machine and the pec deck.

The real rule is narrower than "use the cable one": a partner either holds no fixture at all, so you carry it to the lead lift, or it holds the exact same one. Dumbbells travel. Cables do not.

Three commands now answer this without asking anyone: node scripts/gym-catalogue.mjs for the completeness audit, --options for every way to do every job grouped by where you stand, and --pairing for which blocks send you walking.

Layer oneWhat the goal actually requires

Your goal, plus your ruling that strength wins when strength and size conflict. Four requirements follow and nothing else does:

RequirementBecause
R1Every main lift loadable, and actually going upStrength is the tie-breaker, and strength depends on load
R2Every muscle clears the minimum dose for sizeYou are in a deficit; the floor is keeping what you have
R3The session is finishable in the time you haveYou complete a median of 32% of a prescribed day
R4It does not eat the swim, run and bikeThe hybrid direction you asked for

R3 is a real requirement, not a nicety. A set you never perform contributes nothing to R1 or R2 while still costing you the time to read past it. Ten sessions, best 71%, median 32%: the programme as written describes somebody else's week.

Layer twoThe numbers, and none of them are mine

For strength: 3 to 5 sets a week, per lift

Counted per exercise. Past 5 sets, the meta-analysis says plainly that "additional weekly sets do not consistently enhance strength gains". The floor of 1 set in the same table should not be taken at face value and the authors say so themselves: in trained powerlifters, 1 to 3 weekly sets were insufficient, and 3 to 9 were enough.

Which means

Your main lifts at 4 sets a week are already in the right band. They do not need more. Every earlier instinct to protect them by adding volume was wrong.

For size: 5 to 10 fractional sets a week, per muscle

Counted per muscle, with a set counted as half when the muscle is only assisting. Minimum useful dose is 4. The efficient zone is 5 to 10. From 11 to 18 you need about 8.5 more sets to detect another increment, and it keeps getting worse from there.

Nothing gets worse past 10. It gets inefficient. The cost is paid in R3, in time, and that is the only reason to care.

And in a deficit, extra volume does not buy retention

This is the study that decides the shape of the whole thing. 38 trained men, six weeks in a real deficit, 5 sets per exercise against 3. Lean mass change: −0.51 kg on high volume, −0.92 kg on moderate, p = 0.966. No difference.

Which means

The 41 weekly sets of isolation work are not protecting your muscle while you cut. What protects it is training hard against a load, which R1 already covers.

Pelland 2026, Sports Med 56(2):481-505, 67 studies, 2,058 participants, Tables 3 and 4. Roth 2023, Scand J Med Sci Sports, PMID 36114738, 38 trained males, 30 kcal/kg for 6 weeks at 2.8 g/kg protein.

Layer fiveFour findings, and none is what anyone expected

1. Nothing is missing

Zero muscles are below the minimum dose. Every pass, including my own this morning, went hunting for a hole. There is no hole. The programme covers everything.

2. The defect is surplus, not deficit

Fourteen of sixteen muscles are past the efficient zone. Glutes at 35.5 sit in the second-worst tier, where roughly 12.5 further sets would be needed to detect another increment. Only your erectors and calves land in the 5 to 10 band. And per Roth, that surplus is not buying muscle retention either.

3. The main lifts are correctly dosed and should not be touched

4 sets a week is inside both the powerlifter band and the efficient tier, and five of seven lifts are rising. The one regression is the overhead press, and its cause is an equipment step, not a set count.

4. The surplus is concentrated in work that can never get harder

The single biggest finding

Dead bug 9 sets, glute bridge 8, pushup 6. 23 sets a week, in blocks labelled "main", with no weight column at all.

They cannot serve strength, because they cannot be loaded. They are the sets pushing abs to 26.5 and glutes to 35.5, past the point of useful return. They are pure time. And by your completion data they are disproportionately the sets you already quietly drop.

The question you askedHow each day, each pair, and the week close

This is the part nothing could answer before, and the answer is the per-day columns above rather than a paragraph.

  • Lower A is the knee day. Quads 13, glutes 15.5, adductors 6.5. Back squat leads, frontal-plane work lives here.
  • Lower B is the hip day. Hamstrings 12.5, glutes 16, quads 8 from the front squat and lunges. The RDL leads.
  • The two together give every lower pattern twice a week, which is what the frequency evidence asks for. Both are far past the size target, which is finding 2.
  • Upper A is press-led. Triceps 16, front delts 8, chest 7.
  • Upper B is pull-led. Lats 10, biceps 9.5, upper back 8.5, grip 8.
  • The two together give chest 13, lats 16, upper back 19, and both presses and both pulls twice each.
The whole week

Every one of sixteen muscles above the minimum. Every main pattern twice. Four progressive sets per main lift, and they are going up.

It achieves the goal on R1 and R2 and fails on R3, and R3 is the reason you have never finished a session.

Done, in the order the derivation produced it

Six of seven, and the seventh deliberately not

  1. Take the 23 sets of unloadable work out of the main blocks. Keep one trunk exposure and a glute warm-up, delete the rest biggest lever, fixes R3, costs nothing under R1 or R2
  2. Reverse fly and lateral raise from four days to two, and onto the cable stack 12 sets to 6 puts both inside 5 to 10, and the cable fixes their unreachable weight
  3. Cut the two pairings where the partner shares the lead lift's muscle glute bridge behind the squat, and behind the RDL
  4. Leave every main lift at 4 sets derived, not cautious: more is outside the efficient tier and they are already rising
  5. Fix the overhead press load step the one genuine regression in the programme
  6. Re-source or remove the four unsourced exercises leg curl, calf raise placement, Cable Pallof, pushup
  7. Only then ask whether a second hinge lift is needed and answer it against hamstrings at 21 sets a week

Item 7 is the discipline this whole page exists to impose. The conventional deadlift may well earn a place. It does not earn one today, because hamstrings and glutes are already two of the four most over-served muscles in your week, and adding the heaviest posterior-chain lift there is would push them further past the point where extra sets stop paying.

Cut first. Re-measure. Then see what the week has room for.

Item 7 is the one still open. Hamstrings are at 17 sets a week and glutes at 27.5, so the deadlift still has nowhere to go that does not make that worse. Train the week as it stands, and it gets re-measured against real numbers rather than against a hunch.
